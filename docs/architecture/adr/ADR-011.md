# ADR-011 — Market adapters are region-specific; FERC Order 2222 is not a protocol

## Context

§2.3 states: "FERC Order 2222 is a US regulatory framework whose implementation is mediated through individual RTO/ISO tariffs and requirements. It is not itself a protocol and does not create a universal market API. Regional requirements must be handled by market-specific adapters and configuration." ([R1]) The §2.3 integration table lists "ISO/RTO-specific APIs" as a region-specific market plugin, "not a generic OpenADR mapping". §0 adds that direct ISO/RTO market participation is a region-specific integration, not a universal core function. `03_decision_records.md` (P0.03 §1.2) freezes the standing position as binding on all later phases: **no market adapter may be built on a generic "Order 2222" mapping; a market integration is always a region-specific adapter plugin, selected or configured only after D1 names a jurisdiction and market programme.** D1 remains OPEN, and no Phase 0/1/2 artefact may name a jurisdiction or market as decided (P0.03 §1.4).

Evidence: ledger/phase-00-product-freeze/03_decision_records.md (P0.03 §1.2, §1.4, §1.5 gate record).

## Decision

All market and programme integrations are region-specific adapter plugins (§5.3 market/program adapter category) that translate external market/program semantics into internal events and commitments. No generic "Order 2222" mapping, universal market API, or jurisdiction assumption is built into the core. FERC Order 2222 is treated as what it is — a US regulatory framework implemented through individual RTO/ISO tariffs — never as a protocol. Which region (if any) is integrated first is decision D1, which remains open.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why not chosen |
|---|---|
| Build a generic "FERC Order 2222 adapter" as a universal market API | Forbidden by the frozen standing position (P0.03 §1.2, binding on all later phases): Order 2222 has no protocol to implement — its requirements are mediated through individual RTO/ISO tariffs ([R1]); a "generic" adapter would encode no real tariff and invite false compliance claims (§0.3: universal market qualification is a non-promise). |
| Hard-code one RTO/ISO market integration into the core | Couples the core to one region while D1 is open; §5.3 and the §8.1 `adapters/market-example` layout deliberately keep regions at the seams so that closing D1 adds an adapter, not a core redesign. |
| Defer all market-interface work until D1 closes | Unnecessary: the internal market vocabulary (market signals, market commitments, §15.3 events; the `/api/v1/market-commitments` resource, §15.2) is region-neutral and needed by scheduling and dispatch regardless of region; 0.x simulation phases use simulated market signals, not an ISO/RTO integration (P0.03 §1.4). |

## Consequences

**Positive.** The core stays region-neutral and D1 remains genuinely open; closing D1 becomes an adapter-selection act (plus the D1 ADR required by P0.03 §1.5) rather than a redesign; simulation phases are unblocked by simulated market signals; false qualification implications are structurally avoided.

**Negative.** No out-of-the-box market connectivity exists in 0.x; each regional adapter carries its own maintenance, testing, and conformance burden; market-facing products additionally need region-specific baseline/measurement rules (the D2 baseline-method question, P0.03 §2.3, travels with D1 input).

**Constrains later phases.** No Phase 0/1/2 artefact may name a jurisdiction or market as decided (P0.03 §1.4); market adapters are selected or configured only after D1 closes; implementing an adapter is not a conformance claim, and certified/qualified language requires a test result (§2.3; P0.18 §5).

## Revisit conditions

- When D1 closes, a separate ADR naming the jurisdiction, the RTO/ISO or market programme (if any), and the interconnection/data-residency/liability consequences is filed per the P0.03 §1.5 gate record — this ADR itself needs no change, since region-specific adapters are its thesis.
- Analogous regulatory frameworks in other jurisdictions reinforce rather than reopen this ADR; only a genuine universal market protocol standard would trigger reconsideration.
