## Context

The report's executive decision (§0) defines FlexCore as an open, modular Virtual Power Plant (VPP) orchestration and distribution-aware DER coordination platform: a supervisory orchestration layer over distributed energy resources, not a control-room or protection system (§0, §3.1). §1.6 draws the responsibility boundary explicitly: the site EMS, inverter firmware, BMS, EVSE controller, protection relays, and certified local control systems remain responsible for device-specific safety and fast local control; FlexCore may request an operating point but must not assume the device can or will execute it. §0.3 lists what the first public release and v1.0.0 pilot baseline deliberately do not promise, including hard real-time or protection-grade control and autonomous grid switching. §1.3 excludes ISOs/RTOs seeking a market/control-room replacement, TSOs seeking an EMS/SCADA replacement, and operators seeking a certified protection, relay, or inverter inner-loop controller as initial targets.

Evidence: ledger/phase-00-product-freeze/01_scope_and_non_goals.md (P0.01) freezes this scope, reproduces the §0.3 non-promises verbatim as the scope-creep test for every later phase gate, and reproduces the §1.6 responsibility boundary and §2.1 release ladder in full.

## Decision

FlexCore is a supervisory VPP/DER orchestration and DER-coordination platform. It is not an ADMS/SCADA replacement, not a protective-relay or inverter-inner-loop controller, not an ISO/RTO market platform, and not a hard-real-time controller. Device-specific safety and fast local control remain with site-local certified systems; the entire 0.x line is simulation-only, and field write authority begins at v1.0.0 with operator approval (§0, §2.1).

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why not chosen |
|---|---|
| Position FlexCore as a utility ADMS/DERMS or TSO EMS/SCADA replacement | Explicitly outside the initial target set (§1.3, §0); §1.6 states FlexCore does not replace utility ADMS/EMS/SCADA or market-operator systems. It would import control-room-grade obligations and qualification burdens the report excludes. |
| Build FlexCore as a hard-real-time device or protection controller | Contradicts §0.3 and §14.5: those functions remain in local certified controllers and hardware unless a separate engineered and qualified architecture is developed. It would also contradict the supervisory role in §3.1. |
| Make direct ISO/RTO market participation a universal core function | §0 and §2.3 make market participation a region-specific integration, not a universal core function; recorded further in ADR-011. |

## Consequences

**Positive.** A stable, honest product boundary that the §1.6 responsibility table and the §0.3 non-promises make testable; plugin categories (§5.3) and console display rules (§20.2) can be derived from it; audiences (§1.3) are matched to what the platform actually does.

**Negative.** Buyers needing ADMS, protection, or market-platform functionality in one system are not served; those functions require adjacent systems and integration work; some fast-grid services are out of reach for this product line.

**Constrains later phases.** No later document, demo, or plugin may reintroduce any §0.3 non-promise as a FlexCore capability (P0.01 §2); the §2.1 release ladder's per-release control status is binding; the platform must integrate with, not replace, existing site EMS/ADMS/SCADA systems.

## Revisit conditions

A change to this boundary — for example, funding the "separate engineered and qualified architecture" that §14.5 names as the only path toward real-time or protection functions — would require a new ADR superseding this one, a revised product scope note, and full re-qualification. It cannot happen by incremental feature addition.
