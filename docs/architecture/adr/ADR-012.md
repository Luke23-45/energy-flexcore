## Context

§14.5 states: "The system may implement scheduling and supervisory setpoint delivery, but should not claim primary-frequency, synthetic-inertia, relay, inverter inner-loop, or sub-cycle control capabilities. Those functions remain in local certified controllers and hardware unless a separate engineered and qualified architecture is developed." §1.6 places protective relays, inverter protection, BMS cell-level protection, local droop/grid-forming/inner-loop control, and site PLC/DCS/SCADA safety interlocks outside what FlexCore replaces. §0.3 lists hard-real-time and protection-grade control among the non-promises. §17.5 invariant 10: "Site-local safety and protection remain independent of FlexCore." §21.2 adds the platform invariant that a central service outage must not disable site-local protective controls or force an unsafe device state — which does not make FlexCore a protection layer (P0.20 §3). §17.4 threat 10 lists "no dependence on cloud for protection" as the control for central outage. §7.1 is explicit that Rust does not automatically provide hard real-time behaviour, operational safety, or protocol certification.

Evidence: ledger/phase-00-product-freeze/18_control_authority.md (P0.18) freezes the four control-authority modes, the 0.x simulation-only rule, and the terminology rules; P0.01 (§§2, 4), P0.11 (§8), P0.17 (§§5–6), and P0.20 (§3) freeze the supporting scope, lifecycle, threat-model, and outage invariants.

## Decision

FlexCore makes no hard-real-time and no protection-grade claims in any release, mode, or component. Its control scope is scheduling and supervisory setpoint delivery. Primary frequency response, synthetic inertia, relay protection, inverter inner-loop control, and sub-cycle control remain in local certified controllers and hardware; site-local safety and protection remain independent of FlexCore; a central outage must never disable site-local protective controls or force an unsafe device state.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why not chosen |
|---|---|
| Offer hard-real-time dispatch (tight jitter/deterministic latency guarantees for setpoint delivery) | §14.5: those functions remain in local certified controllers unless a separate engineered and qualified architecture is developed; §0.3 makes it a non-promise; §3.1 defines FlexCore as "not a hard-real-time control system". The supervisory product has no path to this without a different architecture and qualification programme. |
| Implement protection or fast-trip functionality inside FlexCore | §1.6: FlexCore does not replace protective relays, inverter protection, BMS cell-level protection, or site safety interlocks; §17.5 invariant 10 keeps site-local protection independent. FlexCore's command path (acknowledgement is not physical delivery, §14) is structurally unsuited to protection duties. |
| Treat the central platform as the safety layer that sites rely on | §17.4 threat 10 requires site-local fallback and autonomous local EMS with no dependence on cloud for protection; §21.2 makes central-outage independence a platform invariant. A cloud-dependent safety layer would violate both and fail D6 site reviews. |

## Consequences

**Positive.** An honest, stable boundary consistent with the supervisory role (ADR-001); certification and qualification exposure stays where it belongs — in local certified devices and per-site reviews; local safety behaviour does not depend on platform availability or latency.

**Negative.** Fast-grid services and protection-adjacent products are out of scope; some buyers will need adjacent certified systems; "supervisory" must be defended in demos and marketing against over-claiming.

**Constrains later phases.** Every control-authority mode (§21.4) inherits this boundary — including post-1.0 bounded automation; no plugin, document, demo, or console view may re-attach these capabilities to dispatch (P0.11 §8; P0.18 §6); "certified" and "conformant" language requires a test result (P0.18 §5); the entire 0.x line remains simulation-only, with field write authority beginning at v1.0.0 with operator approval.

## Revisit conditions

- Only the §14.5 path exists: funding a separate engineered and qualified architecture for real-time or protection functions would be a new product boundary requiring a new ADR superseding this one, a revised scope note (P0.01), and full re-qualification. Incremental feature work never reaches this boundary by accident.
